# GUE Gaussian Unitary Ensemble — β-Family Wigner-Dyson Spacing Surface

**Blender 5.1 · Python/NumPy · CC0 · Stage Floor · WebXR**

A 128×128 height-field stage floor encoding the Wigner-Dyson family of
nearest-neighbour level-spacing distributions P(s; β) for Dyson index
β ∈ [0, 4]. The x-axis is the normalised level spacing s; the y-axis is
the symmetry class β; the height is P(s; β) normalised by its maximum.
Cobalt colour marks low β (Poisson-like), amber marks high β (GSE, most rigid).

## What you are looking at

Each row of the mesh is a cross-section at a fixed β:
- **β = 0** (front, cobalt): Rayleigh distribution — Wigner formula at
  β→0, no linear repulsion but Gaussian suppression at small s.
- **β = 1** (¼ depth, GOE): P(s) = (π/2)s·exp(−πs²/4). Peak at s ≈ 0.55.
  Orthogonal ensemble (real symmetric matrices), time-reversal invariant.
- **β = 2** (½ depth, GUE): P(s) = (32/π²)s²·exp(−4s²/π). Peak at s ≈ 0.93.
  Unitary ensemble (complex Hermitian), broken time-reversal (e.g. magnetic field).
- **β = 4** (rear, amber, GSE): P(s) = A·s⁴·exp(−Bs²). Peak at s ≈ 1.31.
  Symplectic ensemble (quaternion matrices), spin-orbit coupling without Kramers degeneracy breaking.

The four-fold increase in the power of s (the "level-repulsion exponent") is
not accidental: it counts the real degrees of freedom per off-diagonal matrix
element, which equals the number of independent equations a perturbation must
satisfy to make two levels degenerate.

## Montgomery's conjecture

In 1973 Hugh Montgomery proved (conditionally on GRH) that the pair
correlation of Riemann zeta zeros satisfies
F(α) → 1 − (sinπα/πα)² + δ(α) for α ∈ (0,1). The right-hand side is
exactly the GUE two-point cluster function. Andrew Odlyzko's 1987 numerical
experiments on the 10²²nd zero confirmed this to extraordinary precision.
The conjecture remains unproven but is widely believed to reflect a deep
connection between quantum chaotic systems (Bohigas-Giannoni-Schmit 1984)
and the Riemann zeta function.
