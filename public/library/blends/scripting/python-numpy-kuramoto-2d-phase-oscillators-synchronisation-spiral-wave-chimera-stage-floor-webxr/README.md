### What this is

Yoshiki Kuramoto's 1975 model asks the simplest possible question about
collective behaviour: when do coupled oscillators spontaneously lock
their frequencies? Each oscillator has its own natural frequency,
drawn from a Lorentzian distribution; the only coupling is the sine of
the phase difference to nearest neighbours. Below a critical coupling
K_c, oscillators spin independently — the phase field churns like
turbulence. Above K_c, a macroscopic fraction lock to a common
rhythm, and the cos(θ) height-field shows smooth, slowly rotating
wave-fronts. Right at K_c, spiral wave defects appear — topological
point charges in the phase field, identical in structure to vortices
in the BKT transition of the XY model, but generated by dynamics
rather than thermal fluctuations.

### Physics background

**Mean-field critical coupling**: in the fully connected (all-to-all)
version Kuramoto showed analytically that K_c = 2γ for a Lorentzian of
half-width γ. On the square lattice with nearest-neighbour coupling the
effective threshold shifts to K_c ≈ 1.6 (γ=0.3) — it costs more
coupling to lock across a sparse graph than a dense one.

**Order parameter**: r = |N⁻² Σ exp(iθ_{i,j})| ∈ [0, 1]. For K < K_c,
r → 0 as N → ∞ (incoherent). For K > K_c, r approaches a stable
fixed point. The cos(θ) height-field makes r visible: flat rolling
waves → high r; turbulent salt-and-pepper → low r.

**Spiral waves**: near K_c, phase singularities (points where the
gradient of θ winds by ±2π around a loop) nucleate spontaneously.
They are topological defects — the same mathematical objects as BKT
vortices in the XY equilibrium model, but created by a dynamical
instability rather than thermal fluctuation. Pairs of opposite-charge
spirals annihilate; lone spirals slowly drift.

**Chimera states**: in systems with nonlocal coupling (not just nearest
neighbour), Kuramoto & Battogtokh 2002 discovered that coherent and
incoherent domains can coexist stably — neither fully locked nor fully
turbulent. Abrams & Strogatz 2004 named this a *chimera state*. The
local-order-parameter colour map in this blueprint reveals analogous
transient chimera-like patches in the nearest-neighbour system during
the approach to steady state.
